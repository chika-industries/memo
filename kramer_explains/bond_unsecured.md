### Scene 9: Unsecured Bonds — The Trust Fall

**Setting:** Jerry’s apartment. Jerry is on the couch. George is sitting on the arm of the couch, eating a donut. Kramer bursts in holding a bond certificate and a bungee cord.

**Kramer:** Jerry! George! I’m unsecured!

**George:** (mouth full) You’re what?

**Kramer:** Unsecured! No collateral! No safety net! Just pure, naked credit risk! It’s a trust fall with a coupon!

**Jerry:** You’re holding a bungee cord.

**Kramer:** It’s a metaphor, Jerry! This is a Hikari Tsushin unsecured straight bond. No assets backing it. No land, no buildings, no nothing. Just the full faith and credit of a company that sells office supplies and electricity! The 58th bond! 3.021%! Four years! Ratings A plus and A! They’re practically begging me to take their money!

**George:** So if they go under, you get nothing?

**Kramer:** Not nothing, George! You get the *experience* of being an unsecured creditor! You get in line behind the banks, behind the secured debt, behind the guy who leased them the photocopier! It’s the bankruptcy meet-and-greet!

**Jerry:** And after the secured creditors are done, what’s left?

**Kramer:** A conference table. Maybe a half-used stapler. The Japanese recovery rate on ordinary corporate bonds averages three to four tenths of your principal, George! That’s not nothing! That’s thirty to forty cents on the dollar! It’s a sale!

**George:** (putting down the donut) A sale on your own money. That’s not a sale. That’s a mugging with paperwork.

**Kramer:** No, no, George, you’re missing the beauty! Because it’s unsecured, they pay me *more*! The 59th bond is 3.977% for seven years! They’re paying me extra to trust them! It’s a gratitude coupon!

**Jerry:** It’s a risk premium. They’re paying you because you’re last in line.

**Kramer:** Last in line is still *in line*, Jerry! And Hikari Tsushin is a big company! They just issued ninety-one billion yen of this stuff at 2.52%! Ninety-one billion! That’s not a company that’s going anywhere! That’s a company that’s going *everywhere*!

**George:** (suspicious) Ninety-one billion yen of debt. That’s a lot of trust falls.

**Kramer:** Exactly! It’s a movement! And I’m part of it! I’m an unsecured straight bondholder! I’ve got an inter-bond pari passu clause! Do you know what that means, George?

**George:** It means you’re equally last with everyone else.

**Kramer:** It means I’m *coordinated* in my lastness! It’s a synchronized trust fall!

**Jerry:** So you’re telling me you bought an unsecured bond from a company whose operating cash flow coverage of debt is already a concern, and you’re calling it a safe haven?

**Kramer:** It’s not a safe haven, Jerry. It’s a *faith-based* haven! There’s no collateral! That’s the whole point! If I wanted collateral, I’d buy a secured bond. But secured bonds are for people who don’t believe! I believe!

**George:** (standing up) You know what I believe? I believe if this company hiccups, you’re gonna be standing in a bankruptcy court in Tokyo holding a bungee cord and a coupon for a stapler.

**Kramer:** That’s a risk I’m willing to amortize!

**Jerry:** You can’t amortize a bankruptcy.

**Kramer:** You can if you spread the emotional cost over four years! That’s the 58th bond! Four years! By maturity, I’ll have fully depreciated my optimism!

**George:** (to Jerry) He’s gonna lose everything, isn’t he?

**Jerry:** He’s not losing everything. He’s getting a conference table.

**Kramer:** And a stapler! Don’t forget the stapler!

---

**Quick accuracy check:** An unsecured bond (無担保社債, often called a straight bond in Japan) is backed only by the issuer’s general creditworthiness, not specific collateral. In a default, unsecured bondholders rank behind secured creditors and often behind bank lenders, so recovery can be partial — historically around 30–40% on average for Japanese ordinary corporate bonds, though it varies widely. Hikari Tsushin’s 58th and 59th unsecured bonds were real issuances with coupons of 3.021% and 3.977% respectively, rated A+/A, and included an inter-bond pari passu clause ensuring equal ranking among those bonds. The higher coupon versus secured debt is compensation for that lower standing — a risk premium, not a gratitude payment. So Kramer is right that he’s being paid extra to trust them; he’s just wrong that “unsecured” means “safe.” It means “last in line with a coupon.”
