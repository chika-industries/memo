### Scene 6: Par Value and the Pull to Par

**Setting:** Jerry’s apartment. Jerry is on the couch. Kramer enters carrying a yo-yo, a bond certificate, and a napkin covered in Greek letters and formulas.

**Kramer:** Jerry! I’ve discovered the homing pigeon of finance!

**Jerry:** Is it a pigeon that owes you money?

**Kramer:** No! It’s a bond! Look at this certificate. Par value: $1,000. That’s the face! The coupon is 6% of par — sixty bucks a year — not 6% of what I paid!

**Jerry:** So you finally learned what “par” means.

**Kramer:** I bought it at 80! That means I paid $800 for a thousand-dollar face. It’s a discount bond! And every day, it gets closer to 100 — closer to par — like a yo-yo coming back to my hand! That’s convergence to par!

**Jerry:** Assuming interest rates don’t move and the issuer doesn’t default.

**Kramer:** Assume! Assume! At maturity, the price *has* to be par, because that’s the redemption! Discount bond climbs up. Premium bond slides down. It’s the pull to par, Jerry! The bond gravity!

**Jerry:** It’s not gravity. It’s the remaining cash flows collapsing toward the final principal payment. And if yields change, the yo-yo string gets cut.

**Kramer:** Fine, fine. But look at my napkin! I’ve got the Greeks!

**Jerry:** You’ve got a fraternity mixer on a napkin.

**Kramer:** No! Sensitivity! ΔP over P is approximately minus D-star times Δy, plus one-half convexity times Δy squared! That’s duration and convexity! First-order and second-order sensitivity to interest rate changes! When yields go up, prices go down — D-star tells you how much, convexity tells you how it bends!

**Jerry:** So you understand modified duration.

**Kramer:** I *feel* modified duration. And then there’s theta! Θ equals ∂P/∂t! Sensitivity to time! The aging gremlin! Every day, less time to maturity, and the price rolls toward par!

**Jerry:** Theta is an option Greek. For bonds you’d usually call that pull to par or roll-down. But sure — sensitivity to time.

**Kramer:** And rho! ρ! Sensitivity to interest rates!

**Jerry:** Rho is an option Greek too. Bond people usually just say duration. And vega isn’t even a Greek letter.

**Kramer:** Vega’s a car.

**Jerry:** Vega’s a car.

**Kramer:** But the math works! I bought at 80. My coupon is $60. My current yield is 60 over 800 — 7.5%! But my yield to maturity includes that $200 climb to par! The discount *is* part of the return! That’s convergence, baby!

**Jerry:** So you’re telling me you bought a discount bond, you understand pull to par, and you’ve covered your napkin in second-order Taylor expansion?

**Kramer:** That’s right!

**Jerry:** Then why are you still holding the yo-yo?

**Kramer:** For the premium bond! It goes down to par! Wheeeee!

**Jerry:** Kramer, a premium bond is not a toy. And if rates spike, your “homing pigeon” is gonna fly into a window.

**Kramer:** That’s why I diversified! Half yo-yo, half life preserver!

**Jerry:** That’s not a portfolio. That’s a beach accident.
