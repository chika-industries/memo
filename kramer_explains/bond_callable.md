### Scene 8: Callable Bonds — The Trapdoor Coupon

**Setting:** Jerry’s apartment. Jerry is on the couch. Kramer enters holding a bond certificate with a tiny mousetrap taped to it.

**Kramer:** Jerry! This bond pays 8%! Eight! It’s a dream!

**Jerry:** It’s callable.

**Kramer:** Callable? Like a dog?

**Jerry:** Like a dog that only comes back when it’s convenient for the issuer. The company can redeem it early — usually the second rates fall and they can refinance cheaper.

**Kramer:** So they call my bond?

**Jerry:** They call your bond, they pay you the call price, and you’re standing there with a fistful of cash when rates are low. That’s reinvestment risk. You get evicted when the party gets good.

**Kramer:** But they pay a call premium!

**Jerry:** A dollar and a goodbye.

**Kramer:** No, no — I’ve got five years of call protection! That’s a grace period!

**Jerry:** That’s a five-year grace period before the trapdoor opens. And after that, the issuer decides when you leave.

**Kramer:** Fine. Then I’ll just calculate yield-to-call!

**Jerry:** And yield-to-maturity. And then yield-to-worst.

**Kramer:** Yield-to-worst! That sounds like a dare!

**Jerry:** It’s not a dare. It’s the lowest yield you’d get across the possible call dates and maturity. It’s the “assume the issuer does the thing that’s worst for you” number. Which, with callable bonds, is usually the right assumption.

**Kramer:** So my 8% coupon isn’t really 8%?

**Jerry:** Your coupon is 8%. Your yield depends on when you get your principal back. If they call it early, you get the call price plus the coupon up to then — and then you’re reinvesting at whatever the market gives you. That’s why callable bonds pay more. You’re being paid to hold the trapdoor.

**Kramer:** So it’s a safe haven with a trapdoor!

**Jerry:** It’s not a safe haven. When rates fall, your price doesn’t rally as much because the call caps it. That’s negative convexity. Your bond is allergic to good news.

**Kramer:** So it’s a bond with a mousetrap.

**Jerry:** Yes. And you’re the cheese.

**Kramer:** But if rates rise, they won’t call it! Then I keep my 8%!

**Jerry:** Then you keep your 8% while everybody else is getting 10%. Congratulations — you’re locked in with the cheese.

**Kramer:** So the issuer calls when it’s good for them and leaves me alone when it’s bad for me?

**Jerry:** Now you understand callable bonds. It’s a one-way friendship with a coupon.

**Kramer:** I’m gonna diversify! Half callable, half life preserver!

**Jerry:** That’s not a portfolio. That’s a trapdoor next to a lifeboat.
