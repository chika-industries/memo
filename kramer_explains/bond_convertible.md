### Scene 10: Convertible Bonds — The Mullet Security

**Setting:** Jerry’s apartment. Jerry is on the couch. George is on the arm of the couch, reading a prospectus upside down. Kramer bursts in wearing a reversible jacket — one side pinstripe, one side tracksuit — and holding a bond certificate taped to a stock certificate.

**Kramer:** Jerry! George! I’ve found the perfect security! It’s a bond! It’s a stock! It’s both! It’s the mullet of finance — debt in the front, equity in the back!

**Jerry:** You taped a bond to a stock certificate.

**Kramer:** It’s a convertible bond! I get a coupon, and I get the *right* to convert it into shares! I’m diversified in one certificate! I’m Jekyll and Hyde! I’m a chameleon!

**George:** (squinting) So if the stock goes up, you win. If the stock goes down, you still have the bond. That’s not investing. That’s cheating.

**Kramer:** Exactly! It’s the best of both worlds! The coupon is my safety net, and the conversion is my rocket ship! I’m a rocket ship with a safety net!

**Jerry:** What’s the conversion ratio?

**Kramer:** The what?

**Jerry:** How many shares do you get per bond?

**Kramer:** (flipping the certificate) It says… one thousand shares per bond. Conversion price: fifty dollars.

**Jerry:** So the stock has to get above fifty bucks before conversion makes sense. What’s the stock trading at?

**Kramer:** Forty.

**Jerry:** Then your conversion value is eight hundred dollars. You paid a thousand. You’re paying a conversion premium for the privilege of hoping.

**Kramer:** Hope is a strategy, Jerry!

**George:** Hope is what my uncle used to buy a timeshare in the Poconos. He lost his shirt.

**Kramer:** But if the stock goes to sixty, I convert! I get twelve hundred dollars of stock! I’m a genius!

**Jerry:** And if the stock goes to twenty, you don’t convert. You’re left holding the bond. Which is fine — unless the company’s credit is deteriorating, in which case your “safety net” is a bond floor made of plywood.

**Kramer:** Plywood is a building material, Jerry! It’s sturdy!

**Jerry:** And if the stock goes way up, the issuer calls the bond and forces you to convert. So you get the stock at exactly the moment the company decides it’s done paying you a coupon.

**Kramer:** Forced conversion! Like a surprise party! But with shares!

**George:** (standing up) So let me get this straight. If the stock tanks, you’re stuck with a bond from a company that’s probably in trouble. If the stock soars, they force you to convert and you lose your coupon. So you get the downside of both and the upside of neither?

**Jerry:** He gets a lower coupon than a straight bond, an equity option, and a call feature held by the issuer. It’s not the best of both worlds. It’s the *worst* of both worlds with a nice jacket.

**Kramer:** But George, the coupon is only 1%! That means I’m getting the stock upside for almost free!

**George:** One percent? My savings account pays more than that. My *checking* account pays more than that. My *couch cushions* pay more than that.

**Kramer:** That’s because you don’t believe, George! This is a faith-based security! You’re betting on the stock, but you’re also betting on the credit! It’s a double bet! It’s a parlay!

**Jerry:** It’s a parlay where the bookie can change the odds and hand you a bond instead of a payout.

**Kramer:** Fine. Then I’ll just hold it to maturity.

**Jerry:** Then you never get the equity upside. You just get your principal back — assuming they don’t default. And if they do default, you’re an unsecured creditor. In a reversible jacket.

**George:** (sitting back down) So the jacket’s reversible, but the risk only goes one way. Down.

**Kramer:** That’s why I’m diversifying! Half convertible, half life preserver!

**Jerry:** That’s not a portfolio. That’s a mullet with a lifeboat.

---

**Quick accuracy check:** A convertible bond is a hybrid: it pays a coupon like a bond, but the holder can convert it into a fixed number of shares at a set conversion price/ratio. Conversion value = conversion ratio × stock price; if that’s below the bond’s price, you’re paying a conversion premium for the equity option. Convertibles usually pay a *lower* coupon than straight bonds because the conversion option is valuable to the investor. The “bond floor” depends on credit and rates — if the stock tanks, you’re left with bond-ish exposure and the issuer’s credit risk. If the stock soars, issuers often call the bond to force conversion, ending the coupon. So it’s not “best of both worlds”; it’s a coupon plus an equity option plus issuer call features and credit risk. The mullet metaphor stands: debt in the front, equity in the back — and the issuer decides when it’s time for a haircut.
