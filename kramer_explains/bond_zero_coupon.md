### Scene 7: STRIPS and Zero-Coupon Bonds

**Setting:** Jerry’s apartment. Jerry is on the couch. Kramer bursts in holding a bond certificate, a pair of scissors, and a roll of masking tape.

**Kramer:** Jerry! I’m stripping!

**Jerry:** I don’t want to know. I don’t want to hear it. I don’t want to smell it.

**Kramer:** No! STRIPS! Separate Trading of Registered Interest and Principal of Securities! I’m stripping the coupons off this bond!

**Jerry:** You just cut up a financial instrument with arts-and-crafts scissors.

**Kramer:** It’s a metaphor, Jerry! The coupon payments get separated from the principal. Each coupon becomes its own little zero-coupon bond. The principal becomes another zero. No interest payments! Just one big payment at maturity!

**Jerry:** So you’ve invented a bond that does nothing for thirty years and then taps you on the shoulder.

**Kramer:** Exactly! You buy it at a deep discount — say 40 cents on the dollar — and it accretes to par! It climbs to 100! No coupons along the way! It’s the strong, silent type of bonds!

**Jerry:** And that’s your safe haven?

**Kramer:** Even safer! There’s no interest payment to miss! No coupon check to bounce! The default risk is *masked*!

**Jerry:** Ah. There it is.

**Kramer:** What?

**Jerry:** “Masked.” Kramer, no coupon payments don’t eliminate default risk. They hide the early-warning signal. With a coupon bond, if the issuer misses a payment, everybody sees it. With a corporate zero, the issuer doesn’t have to write a check until maturity. The risk is back-loaded. It’s not masked — it’s wearing a little mask and waiting by the door.

**Kramer:** So it’s a stealth default!

**Jerry:** For a corporate zero, yes. The balloon payment at maturity is the moment of truth. Until then, the issuer can look fine because there’s no coupon to miss. That’s the “mask.”

**Kramer:** But STRIPS are Treasuries! Full faith and credit! No credit risk!

**Jerry:** Correct. STRIPS are Treasury zeros. The credit-risk mask isn’t the issue there. The issue is interest-rate risk. No coupons means all the cash comes at the end, so duration is huge. When rates move, your “strong, silent bond” does the hully-gully.

**Kramer:** I got a Greek for that! Ω!

**Jerry:** That’s omega. Not a bond Greek.

**Kramer:** It’s the end! It’s the final payment!

**Jerry:** And there’s another little surprise: you pay tax on the imputed interest every year even though you get no cash. Phantom income.

**Kramer:** Phantom income! Like a ghost paycheck!

**Jerry:** Yes. And your ghost paycheck is due in April.

**Kramer:** So my zero has no coupons, no early default signal, huge duration, and phantom taxes?

**Jerry:** And it converges to par at maturity. You got the pull to par right.

**Kramer:** So it’s safe-ish!

**Jerry:** It’s *Treasury* safe-ish on credit and *roller coaster* unsafe on rates. And your corporate zero is a default risk in a tuxedo waiting until the last dance.

**Kramer:** So I should diversify! Half STRIPS, half life preserver!

**Jerry:** That’s not a portfolio. That’s a magic act with a tax bill.
